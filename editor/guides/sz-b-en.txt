Sample Undergraduate Thesis (Design) --Science and Engineering

The undergraduate thesis (design) is an academic paper of research value and practical significance, submitted by a student prior to graduation. It represents an initial endeavor by undergraduates to engage in engineering design, scientific experimentation, and research activities. The thesis is a scientific presentation of the outcomes achieved by students under the guidance of their supervisors, and serves as a key basis for determining students' eligibility for graduation and the conferral of degrees. The writing of the undergraduate thesis (design) constitutes a fundamental component of undergraduate training. Supervisors are expected to provide close guidance and enforce strict quality control throughout the process.
The content of the undergraduate thesis (design) shall be factual and truthful. Any act of fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism is strictly prohibited. The thesis must comply with relevant national and industry-specific standards, as well as the grammatical and stylistic conventions of the Chinese language.
1. Content Requirements
1.1 Title
The title must clearly, accurately, and appropriately reflect the core and specific focus of the thesis or dissertation, using concise language—generally limited to no more than 25 Chinese characters. It should be provided in both Chinese and English, typically formatted as a noun phrase. Avoid uncommon abbreviations, acronyms, symbols, codes, or formulas whenever possible.
If the content cannot be succinctly expressed in a single title, a subtitle may be added, with the combined length not exceeding 35 Chinese characters. In Chinese, separate the main title and subtitle with a two-em dash (——); in English, use a colon (:). No other punctuation marks are allowed. The subtitle should serve to supplement or clarify the main title. The main title and subtitle must remain consistent throughout the thesis.
1.2 Abstract and Keywords
1.2.1 Abstract
The abstract is a concise summary of the thesis content and should be independent and self-contained, meaning that readers can understand the essential information of the entire thesis without reading the full text. The abstract should include the purpose of the research, its theoretical and practical significance, the main research content, and research methods, with emphasis on the research results and conclusions.
The abstract should be complete, objective, and accurate, ensuring no omissions, exaggerations, or additions. It should be written paragraph by paragraph in a logical sequence, avoiding a table-of-contents-style introduction. In describing the research content, methods, and main conclusions, the abstract should generally be written in the third person, using expressions such as "analyzed the reasons for...", "it is believed that...", and "an exploration was conducted on...", except where the author's value judgments and experiential assessments may be expressed in the first person. Subjective evaluative comments and excessive descriptions of background, purpose, significance, concepts, and general (common knowledge) theories should be avoided.
The abstract should use standardized terminology (including place names, institution names, and personal names). For new terms or terms without Chinese translations, the foreign term may be used, or the Chinese translation may be followed by the foreign term in parentheses. Formulas, chemical structures, figures, tables, uncommon abbreviations, and non-publicly recognized symbols and terms should not be used in the abstract, and citation numbers should not be marked.
1.2.2 Keywords
Keywords are subject headings for retrieval purposes. They should concisely reflect the distinguishing features of the thesis and the essence of the research results. They should have semantic meaning, have clear sources within the thesis, and should, as much as possible, adopt standard terms from the Chinese Thesaurus or discipline-specific thesauri. Three to six keywords should be listed, arranged in descending order according to the scope of denotation (from broad to narrow).
1.3 Table of Contents
The table of contents is a sequential list of all chapters and sections of the thesis. It should form a separate page and include all level-1 (chapter), level-2 (section), and level-3 (subsection) headings along with their respective page numbers.
1.4 Main Body of the Thesis
The main body includes the introduction, the main text, and the conclusion.
1.4.1 Introduction
The introduction generally serves as Chapter 1. It should include: the source, background, and theoretical and practical significance of the research topic; the research progress and findings in related fields at home and abroad, as well as existing deficiencies or issues requiring further in-depth investigation; and a summary of the theoretical analytical framework, research content, research procedures, and methods to be employed.
In the introduction, attention should be paid to accurately marking the cited domestic and international literature. The description of the main research content should preferably be written in the future tense, and the thesis table of contents should not be directly used as the research content.
1.4.2 Main Text
The main text is the principal part of the undergraduate thesis (design) and should be logically rigorous, well-structured, clearly focused, and written in concise and fluent language. The various chapters should be interconnected, forming an organic whole. The data presented in the thesis must be authentic and reliable, the reasoning correct, and the conclusions clear, free from conceptual and scientific errors. The conditions, experimental processes, and simulation processes for scientific experiments and computer simulations should be described in detail, rather than presenting results, curves, and conclusions directly. When citing the research findings of others or adopting the results of others, the source must be indicated, and such content must not be conflated with the theoretical analyses proposed by the author.
At the end of each chapter of the main text, there should be a "Chapter Summary" section, except for chapters on experimental methods or materials where such a summary may be omitted. The chapter summary is a concise and accurate summary of the research content, methods, and findings of each chapter, and also serves as the basis for the final conclusion of the thesis.
1.4.3 Conclusion
The conclusion, as an integral part of the main body of the undergraduate thesis (design), should be presented on a separate page, without a chapter number, and without marked citations.
The conclusion should be a concise summary of the innovative results achieved by the author during the research process of the graduation thesis (design), and should not be confused with the abstract. The conclusion should generally be 400–2,000 Chinese characters in length.
1.5 References
All cited references must be included in the reference list and numbered sequentially in the order in which they appear; however, each source should be assigned only one number.
Primary sources should be cited whenever possible. When primary sources cannot be cited, both the secondary source and the primary source should be marked.
The undergraduate thesis (design) should generally include no fewer than 15 references, of which no fewer than 3 should be in a foreign language (specific requirements may be adjusted for special majors as deemed appropriate). References published within the past five years should generally account for no less than one-third of the total, and references from the past two years should be included.
Product manuals and unpublished research reports (with the exception of well-known internal reports such as PB and AD reports, and corporate technical reports of major companies) are generally not suitable for inclusion as references.
When citing online references, the precise web address of the document should be indicated. Online references and various standards are not counted within the prescribed number of references stipulated above. Papers published by the author during the period of undergraduate studies should not be included in the reference list.
1.6 Innovative Achievements During Bachelor's Studies
Following the undergraduate thesis (design), a list should be provided of academic papers (including those already accepted for publication) published by the undergraduate student during the period of bachelor's studies that are related to the content of the graduation thesis (design). Other research achievements and patents obtained during the bachelor's studies may be listed separately as a separate item. Academic papers unrelated to the graduation thesis (design), and academic papers for which the author is listed as the second author or later (excluding cases where the first author is the supervisor or co-supervisor), should not be listed here.
1.7 Copyright Statement and Authorization Letter
The author may download the electronic version of this section directly from the Office of Academic Affairs website. The author and the supervisor must sign their names personally.
1.8 Acknowledgements
Acknowledgements should be made to supervisors, to organizations and individuals who provided guidance or assistance in the completion of the graduation thesis (design), and to sponsors who provided funding for the research. The content should be simple and modest, and the language should be reserved and graceful.

2. Writing Specifications
2.1 Word Count of Main Body
Each major should establish word count requirements for undergraduate thesis (design) that are commensurate with its graduation requirements, in accordance with the National Standards for Teaching Quality of Undergraduate Majors in General Higher Education Institutions. The main body of the thesis should generally be no less than 15,000 Chinese characters (including figures and tables).
2.2 Thesis Writing
The undergraduate thesis (design) must be typed, formatted, and printed on a computer.
Page numbers should be centered below the lower margin of the text block. Page numbers for front matter sections such as the abstract, table of contents, and list of physical quantities and symbols should be numbered separately using Roman numerals, while page numbers from the main body onward should be numbered using Arabic numerals.
2.3 Abstract and Keywords
The abstract of the undergraduate thesis (design) is required to be presented in both Chinese and English. The Chinese abstract should generally be around 300 Chinese characters in length, with the principle being that it adequately presents the required content. The language should be concise, and the paragraphs should flow smoothly. The English abstract should be completely consistent with the Chinese abstract in content, and should be grammatically and lexically accurate. The abstract page does not need to include the thesis title.
Keywords should be listed below the abstract. The Chinese and English keywords should correspond one-to-one and be placed below the Chinese and English abstracts respectively. Chinese keywords should be separated by semicolons (；), while English keywords should be separated by commas (,).
The Chinese and English abstracts should each occupy a separate page, with the Chinese version presented first, followed by the English version.
2.4 Table of Contents
The table of contents should include all level-1 (chapter), level-2 (section), and level-3 (subsection) headings and their page numbers, including:
	•	Abstract
	•	Abstract (English)
	•	List of Physical Quantities and Symbols (refer to Appendix 1; this section may be omitted if national standard symbols are used)
	•	Chapter Headings of the Main Body (up to level 3, i.e., ×.×.×)
	•	Conclusion
	•	References
	•	Appendices
	•	Innovative Achievements During Bachelor's Studies
	•	Copyright Statement
	•	Authorization Letter
	•	Acknowledgements
2.5 Main Body
2.5.1 Chapters and Chapter Titles
The main body of the thesis should be written in chapters, with each chapter beginning on a new page.
Chapter and section titles should be concise, clear, and focused, generally not exceeding 15 Chinese characters, and should not contain punctuation marks. English abbreviations should be avoided as much as possible in titles; where they are necessary, commonly used abbreviations in the relevant industry should be used.
2.5.2 Heading Levels
The number of heading levels should be kept to a minimum and determined according to actual needs. The recommended heading level format is shown in Table 1 in Section 3.7 of this document.
Heading levels should be consistent throughout. If content under a section does not require further subdivision, items may be listed directly. The specific level to be used depends on the need.

2.6 Citation and Referencing
All citations must comply with the “Information and Documentation—Rules for Bibliographic References and Citations to Information Resources”（GB/T 7714—2015）and use a sequential numbering format. In the main body, citation numbers should appear as superscript Arabic numerals in small font size 4, enclosed in square brackets “[ ]”, and placed immediately after the last character of the cited text. Citation formatting requirements:
(1) When citing a single source, format it as follows: “re-milling [1]”.
(2) When citing multiple references at a single point, include all reference numbers within one set of square brackets, separated by commas. Use a hyphen to indicate a continuous range.
Example: “...formed multiple mathematical models [7,9,11-13]...”
(3) When citing different parts of the same reference, place the page number of the cited text immediately after the reference number, outside the square brackets.
Example: “...determination of CAMP content in interstitial cells [3]101-108... method for content determination [3]92...”
(4) When directly mentioning reference sources in the text, place the reference numbers in-line with the main text in small font size 4. Example: “As shown in references [8,10–13] …”.
Citation marks must not be placed at any heading level.
2.7 Terminology
Technical terms and names of equipment and components should adopt the terminology or names prescribed in national or ministerial standards. For terms not covered by standards, commonly used industry terminology or names should be used. Terminology must be consistent throughout the thesis; any special terms or newly coined terms should be explained or annotated in appropriate places.
When using English abbreviations, except for widely used industry-standard abbreviations, the first occurrence of each abbreviation in the text should be accompanied by the full English term in parentheses.
2.8 Physical Quantity Notation
2.8.1 Names and Symbols of Physical Quantities
The names and symbols of physical quantities should comply with the provisions of SI Units and Their Applications (GB 3100—93) and Quantities and Units (GB 3102.1~13—93). The name and symbol of a given physical quantity should be used consistently throughout the thesis.
Symbols for physical quantities must be written in italics.
2.8.2 Units of Measurement for Physical Quantities
The units and symbols for physical quantities should comply with the Legal Units of Measurement of the People's Republic of China (promulgated by the State Council in 1984; see Appendix 1), as well as SI Units and Their Applications (GB 3100—93) and Quantities and Units (GB 3102.1~13—93). Non-legal units and symbols are prohibited. Units may be expressed in Chinese characters or symbols, but usage should be consistent throughout. Unit symbols should be in lowercase letters, except for the first letter of units named after persons, which should be capitalized.
For non-physical units (e.g., 件 [pieces], 台 [sets], 人 [persons], 元 [yuan], 次 [times]), a combination of Chinese characters and unit symbols is allowed—for example, “万t·km” or “t/(人·a)”.
When expressing vague quantities, it is acceptable to use Chinese terms such as “几千克 (several kilograms).
Time should be expressed using Chinese units—e.g., “上午8点3刻” (8:45 a.m.)—rather than in international notation like “8h45min”.
Unit symbols should always be in roman (upright) type.
2.9 Roman and Italic Usage for Foreign Letters
In accordance with the provisions of SI Units and Their Applications (GB 3100—93) and Quantities and Units (GB 3102.1~13—93), symbols for physical quantities, physical constants, and variables should be in italics, while unit symbols and other symbols should be in roman (upright) type. Foreign letters should be set in Times New Roman font.
2.10 Numbers
In accordance with General Rules for Writing Numbers in Publications (GB/T 15835—2011), Arabic numerals (see Appendix 2) should generally be used, except where Chinese numerals are customary. Times New Roman font should be used.
2.11 Formulas
Formulas in the thesis should begin on a new line, be centered, and be clearly distinguished from the surrounding text by appropriate spacing. Formulas should be numbered sequentially, with the number placed in parentheses. Formula numbers should be organized by chapter, e.g., the first formula in Chapter 1 would be numbered "(1-1)". Formula numbers should be right-aligned.
When citing a formula in the text, expressions such as "see formula (1-1)" or "from formula (1-1)" are generally used.
If text appears before a formula (e.g., "Solution", "Assume"), the text should be indented by 4 half-width spaces, while the formula remains centered; no punctuation should follow the formula.
Use parentheses when writing division expressions with slashes to avoid ambiguity, for example, . Typically, the multiplication is placed first—for example,  is preferred over .
If a formula is too long to fit on one line, it should preferably be broken at the equal sign (=). If this is not possible, it may be broken at operators such as +, −, ×, or ÷. The operator should appear at the beginning of the new line, not at the end of the previous one.
When a physical quantity symbol appears for the first time in a formula, it must be annotated. If the annotation extends to a second line, the continuation should align with the first character following the dash (——), which should occupy the space of four half-width characters. If the annotation includes a formula, do not assign it a separate formula number, for example, (3-1). Formatting example:

2.12 Tables
Tables should be self-explanatory. Tables should not have left or right border lines. The recommended format for tables is the internationally accepted three-line table. If the three-line table is insufficient to clearly present the content, column spacing should be increased as needed, with clarity being the primary consideration, as shown in Table 2 of Appendix 1. Text within tables should use SimSun font for Chinese and Times New Roman for English/numbers. The font size should preferably be 5 (small 5 may be used when content is extensive, but font size should be consistent within a single table).
Each table should have a caption (consisting of a table number and a table title). Table numbers are generally organized by chapter, e.g., the first table in Chapter 1 would be numbered "Table 1-1". The table number and title should be separated by 2 half-width spaces. Punctuation marks are not permitted in the table title, and no punctuation should follow the title. The table caption should be placed above the table, using Chinese only, in SimSun font size 5.
Table headers should be simple and clear, and diagonal lines should be avoided as much as possible. Chemical symbols or physical quantity symbols may be used in table headers.
If the entire table uses the same unit, the unit symbol should be moved to the upper right corner of the table header and enclosed in parentheses.
Data in tables should be accurate and clearly written. Empty cells should be filled with an em dash "—" (occupying 2 half-width characters). Where text or numbers are identical vertically or horizontally, the column merging method should be used; the use of symbols such as "〃" or "同上" (same as above) is not permitted.
For textual descriptions within tables, the first line should be indented by 2 half-width spaces, and subsequent lines should be flush with the left margin, with no punctuation at the end of sentences.
Relevant textual prompts must appear before a table in the text, such as "see Table 1-1" or "as shown in Table 1-1". Generally, tables should not be split across two pages. If a table cannot fit on one page, it may be continued on the next page, with the page number and "(continued)" indicated in the upper right corner, and the table header repeated. A blank line should be inserted between the table and the surrounding text.
When citing tables from literature, the reference number should be marked at the upper right corner of the table title in addition to being marked in the main text.
2.13 Figures
Figures should be self-explanatory. Figures should be closely integrated with the text, consistent with the content, and accurate. Figures and photographs should be selected concisely and should be complete and clear.
Mechanical engineering drawings: The first-angle projection method should be used, strictly in accordance with the relevant provisions of Technical Drawings – General Principles of Presentation – Basic Conventions for Leader Lines and Reference Lines (GB/T 4457.2—2003), Mechanical Drawings – Graphical Symbols for Kinematic Diagrams (GB/T 4460—2013), Technical Drawings – Simplified Representation (GB/T 16675.1~2—2012), Geometrical Product Specifications (GPS) – Indication of Surface Texture in Technical Product Documentation (GB/T 131—2006), and Mechanical Engineering CAD Drawing Rules (GB/T 14665—2012).
Data flowcharts, program flowcharts, system flowcharts, etc.: Should comply with Information Processing – Documentation Symbols and Conventions for Data Flowcharts, Program Flowcharts, System Flowcharts, Program Network Charts, and System Resource Charts (GB/T 1526—1989).
Electrical diagrams: Graphical symbols and letter symbols should comply with the relevant standards listed in Appendix 3.
Flowcharts: Structured programming methods must be adopted, and flowchart boxes must be used correctly.
For graphics without prescribed symbols, common practices in the relevant industry should be used.
Grid lines in coordinate graphs should be drawn as thin solid lines, with line weight not exceeding that of the curves; coordinate graphs with numerical labels must indicate the coordinate units.
Photographs should have clearly defined subjects and prominent contours for easy reproduction. If reproductions are enlarged or reduced, they must be clear with moderate contrast. Photographs should include a scale indicating the dimensions of the object.
When citing figures from literature, the reference number should be marked at the upper right corner of the figure title in addition to being marked in the main text.
2.13.1 Figure Captions and Explanations
Each figure should have a caption (consisting of a figure number and a figure title). Punctuation marks should be avoided in figure titles. The figure title should be placed after the figure number, separated by 2 half-width spaces. Figure numbers should be organized by chapter, e.g., the first figure in Chapter 1 would be numbered "Figure 1-1". The caption should be placed below the figure, written in Chinese only, using SimSun font size 5. Figure notes or other explanations should be placed above the figure title. When citing a figure, the source should be indicated by placing the reference number at the upper right corner of the figure title. For figures with sub-figures, sub-figure captions should be placed below the respective sub-figure or below the main figure title, and may be written in Chinese only. Sub-figure labels should use (a), (b), etc., and should be listed consecutively below the main figure title, separated by semicolons (;).
Explanatory text within figures should be in Chinese (except for cited foreign-language figures) or numeric symbols. Textual explanations should be placed above the figure title (or above the sub-figure captions, if applicable).
Text within figures should use SimSun font for Chinese and Times New Roman for English/numbers. Font size should preferably be 5 (small 5 may be used when content is extensive, based on clarity, but font size should be consistent within a single figure). Text usage should be consistent within the same figure. Physical quantities and symbols in figures and tables should be italicized.
2.13.2 Figure Arrangement
Before a figure appears, the text must include a prompt referring to the figure, such as "see Figure 1-1" or "as shown in Figure 1-1". The figure and its caption form a single unit and must not be separated across two pages. If there is insufficient space on the page to accommodate the entire figure, the text following may be moved forward, and the figure shifted to the next page. For figures with many sub-figures that cannot fit on one page, they may be continued on the next page; the main figure title should appear only on the first page, and subsequent pages should be marked with "Figure ×-× (continued)".
Appropriate spacing should be maintained above and below the figure in relation to the surrounding text.

2.15 Appendices
Appendices serve as supplementary material to the main body and are not mandatory.
The following types of content may be included as appendices:
(1) Materials that are necessary for the completeness of the thesis but would compromise the logical flow and organization if included in the main text, including detailed information, more in-depth descriptions of research methods and techniques, and supplementary information useful for understanding the main text.
(2) Materials that, due to length or reproduction issues, are not suitable for inclusion in the main text.
(3) Rare or valuable materials that are not suitable for inclusion in the main text.
(4) Materials that are not necessary reading for the general reader but are of reference value to peers in the field.
(5) Important raw data, mathematical derivations, structural diagrams, statistical tables, self-developed computer programs, computer printouts, etc.
2.16 Innovative Achievements During Bachelor's Studies
The format for listing innovative achievements should follow the same rules as for references, with the addition of the corresponding chapter/section number of the thesis after the page number. For academic papers that have been published and indexed by EI or SCI, the accession number should be indicated. For SCI papers, the impact factor of the journal at the time of publication should generally be noted. For academic papers that have been accepted but not yet published, please indicate whether the journal is an EI or SCI source.
2.17 Book Spine
To facilitate the management of undergraduate thesis (design), it is recommended to follow the provisions of Spine Rules for Books and Other Publications (GB/T 11668—1989), printing the thesis title and the name of the degree-granting institution on the book spine in small font size 4, using bold type.
2.18 Other
Centuries must be indicated before year designations, e.g., the 1970s (20th century).

Chinese fonts used in the thesis (except for headings at various levels) should be SimSun; headings at various levels should use SimHei (bold) font. Numbers and English text in the thesis should use Times New Roman font.

Appendix 2 Guidelines for the Use of Numbers
According to the “General Rules for Writing Numerals in Publications ” (GB/T 15835—2011), Arabic numerals should generally be used, except in cases where Chinese numerals are conventionally preferred.
(1) Centuries, decades, years, months, days, and times based on the Gregorian calendar may be written using either Arabic numerals or Chinese characters—for example, “20世纪” or “二十世纪” (20th century), “20世纪80年代” or “二十世纪八十年代” (1980s), “4时3刻” or “四时三刻” (4:45). Years must be written in full four-digit format (e.g., “1989年” rather than “89年”). Use of Arabic numerals is generally recommended.
(2) Numbers used for counting or calculation—including integers, fractions, decimals, percentages, and approximations—should be written in Arabic numerals. Examples: 3/4, 4.5%, 10个月 (10 months), 500多种 (over 500 types).
(3) The choice between Chinese and Arabic numerals depends on context. Single digits not related to scientific or statistical data may be written in Chinese numerals, e.g., “一个人” (one person) or “六条意见” (six opinions). Days of the week should always be written in Chinese numerals, for example, “星期六” (Saturday). When indicating an approximate range with consecutive numbers, use Chinese numerals without a dunhao (、), for example: “三五天” (three to five days), “七八十种” (seventy to eighty types), “四十五六岁” (forty-five to forty-six years old), and “一千七八百元” (one thousand seven to eight hundred yuan).
(4) When numbers function as morphemes in fixed words, phrases, idioms, or abbreviations, Chinese numerals should be used, for example: 二倍体 (diploid), 三叶虫 (trilobite), 第三世界 (the Third World), “七五”规划 (the Seventh Five-Year Plan), and idioms like 相差十万八千里 (literally “differ by 108,000 li”, meaning “worlds apart”).
(5) For numbers with five or more digits and many trailing zeros, it is acceptable to use the units 万 (ten thousand) or 亿 (hundred million). However, units such as 十 (ten), 百 (hundred), 千 (thousand), 十万 (one hundred thousand), 百万 (million), 千万 (ten million), 十亿 (billion), 百亿 (ten billion), and 千亿 (hundred billion) should generally not be used. For example, 345,000,000千米 (345,000,000 kilometers) can be written as 3.45亿千米 or 34,500万千米, but not as 3亿4,500万千米 or 3亿4千5百万千米.
(6) When writing numbers, it is not necessary to allocate one digit per grid space; two digits can typically fit within a single grid. Do not use commas to separate digit groups. For numbers with four or more digits, insert a half-digit space (approximately one-quarter the width of a Chinese character) every three digits, counting from the right. For example, write “3 000 000” instead of “3,000,000”. In decimal numbers, apply the same spacing rule to the digits to the right of the decimal point, grouping them in sets of three. Multi-digit Arabic numerals must not be split across lines.
(7) When expressing changes in quantity, be precise to avoid ambiguity. Please pay attention to the following distinctions:
① “增加为（或增加到）过去的二倍” — “increased to twice the original amount”: Original = 1 → Now = 2;
② “增加（或增加了）二倍” — “increased by twice the original amount” (i.e., tripled): Original = 1 → Now = 3;
③ “超额80%” — “exceeded by 80%”: Target = 100 → Actual = 180;
④ “降低到80%” — “decreased to 80% of the original amount): Original = 100 → Now = 80;
⑤ “降低（或降低了）80%” — “decreased by 80% of the original amount”: Original = 100 → Now = 20;
⑥ “为原数的1/4” — “1/4 of the original amount”: Original = 4 → Now = 1.
Note: When expressing decreases in quantity, avoid using phrases like “decreased by two times”. Use clear fractions instead, such as “reduced to 1/2” or “reduced to 1/3 of the original amount”.
